## Effects

**Consistent Expectations turns expensive goods into a 1-coin lottery: the higher the sticker price, the lower each success chance.**

For an item whose original price is **N** coins:

- each attempt costs **1** coin;
- success chance is **1 / N**;
- success buys the item normally;
- failure still spends that 1 coin, but the goods stay on the shelf for another try.

Example with sticker price **15**:

> each attempt costs 1 coin with about a **6.7%** chance to succeed.

If each try succeeds with probability `1 / N`, the average number of tries is about **N**. In an ideal unlimited-retry case, a price-N item still costs about **N** coins on average—the trinket reshapes risk and cash-flow, not the mathematical expectation.

In rooms that can start an ambush (such as Challenge Rooms), a failed purchase may also begin the ambush.

## Tips

- **Even a few coins can try goods that would normally be unaffordable.** Failed attempts still spend coins, so this is a chance to gamble early—not a reliable discount.
